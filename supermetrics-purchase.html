Supermetrics · Purchase journey redesign · 2024

A self-serve purchase flow driving 22% ARR growth

I redesigned how customers choose and buy Supermetrics, moving the purchase journey into the product and around a flexible new package model.

Role
Senior Product Designer
Timeline
6 months, idea to launch
Team
Cross-functional, with two fellow designers on the concept
Scope
Research, UX, UI, testing

Context

Supermetrics pulls marketing and sales data from many platforms into one source of truth. A move to a new subscription model meant the way people bought it had to change too.

Impact

Self-serve buying grew ARR by 22%.

The problem

Buying Supermetrics was complex, rigid and sales-led.

The existing flow held customers back in three ways.

  • Choosing a product was complex, and customers were unsure which data sources they needed
  • It was inflexible, limited to a single destination with awkward upgrade paths
  • It leaned heavily on sales, keeping customers in a sales-led journey

Research

Customers wanted flexible packages and prices they could see.

Mapping the current destination purchase flows showed the disjointed system could not support the new model. We expected customers to prefer packages for their flexibility, customisation and fairer pricing, but knew unclear pricing or hidden value could put new customers off.

I interviewed ten customers across segments and analysed the sessions with affinity mapping and Vertex AI.

  • Customers liked being able to customise packages, and valued TikTok access in the first two tiers
  • Every customer was willing to buy at the prices shown. Two needed a business case to move up a tier, and agencies valued the middle tier's higher limits
  • The data source picker caused confusion, from its category patterns to a search bar that was not always visible
  • Six of ten knew of useful features they could not reach in self-serve or trial

Process

From a shared concept to a tested, shipped flow.

I shaped the concept with a Principal and a Senior Product Designer, then took ownership and led research and delivery with the wider team. We moved purchasing into the hub, simplified package customisation, and tested the flows and mockups in Maze.

The solution

Customers now build and buy their own package in the hub.

Buying moved into the hub as a self-serve flow built around flexible packages. Customers pick a package, tailor its destinations, data sources and add-ons with the price always in view, then pay without talking to sales.

Outcome

More customers, fewer tickets and 22% more revenue.

Maze testing showed the flow worked well, with data source selection the clearest place to refine further. After launch it grew ARR by 22% and lifted conversion for paid self-serve customers.

Reflections

Transparent pricing is what made self-serve work.

Moving the purchase into the hub only worked because pricing became transparent: customers trusted a flow they could see the whole cost of.

  • Testing early in Maze showed data source selection was the hardest step, and it stayed the clearest place to keep improving
  • Shaping the concept with senior designers, then owning research and delivery, kept the vision and the details connected
  • Next, I would test pricing language with new customers sooner, since unclear value was the biggest risk we saw in research
